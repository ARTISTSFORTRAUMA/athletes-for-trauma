“Respecting the past while creating the future.”

Building on a legacy · The Galt House Hotel

Interview Tattiahna WelshPhotography Sarah AnneDesign Per Bernal

The Galt House Hotel Chief Marketing Officer, Lance George, is preserving the history of the hotel while helping shape its future through new experiences like ONI Fights 5: MMA.

The Galt HouseA vision bigger than hospitality

The Galt House was built around a vision bigger than hospitality. Founder Al J. Schneider saw Louisville’s abandoned downtown waterfront and imagined a destination that could bring people, business, and energy back to the city. Since joining the company in 2019, George has helped carry that legacy forward.

During the hotel’s transformational renovation, 65 cents of every dollar spent stayed within the local community, supporting local businesses, workers, and families. For George, carrying that legacy forward means finding the balance between “respecting the past while creating the future.”

“Filling the hotel is about more than occupancy or revenue.”

The people behind itA deeper purpose

At the Galt House, that philosophy gives the business a deeper purpose. For George, it means a housekeeper gets the hours needed, a front desk employee gets another shift, and a team member can provide for their family.

It is why his work has remained personal: the success of the hotel can create opportunities for the people behind it while introducing visitors to the Kentucky he is proud to call home.

TRUST AgencyThinking differently

That willingness to think differently has also shaped George’s relationship with Eddie Donaldson of TRUST Agency. Their connection began in collaboration with Artists for Trauma and an early idea for a Muhammad Ali mural on the side of the hotel.

The mural by Shepard Fairey ultimately found its home at the Chestnut Street YMCA, but the collaboration continued. High-end art was brought into the Galt House, and the relationship developed into a creative partnership built around finding new ways to tell the stories of the hotel and Louisville.

ONI Fights 5: MMAWhat could it become?

On October 10, 2026, George is helping bring a new kind of experience to the Galt House Hotel as a ballroom transforms into a cage-fighting arena for ONI Fights 5: MMA. Presented in partnership with TRUST Agency, the night will be headlined by Isaiah “The Vanilla Gorilla” Ferguson vs. Chris Johnson.

The hotel’s first MMA event reflects an approach that honors the Galt House’s history without allowing that history to define its limits.

“We don’t simply ask, ‘What is this space designed for?’ We ask, ‘What could it become?’”
Lance George

Marion CountySome pretty big things

George’s approach is rooted in Marion County, a small town in Kentucky that, as he puts it, “has given the world some pretty big things.”

Marion County is a place shaped by community, where people wave as they pass on the road, conversations come easily, and neighbors step up when someone needs help. It is also home to Maker’s Mark bourbon, Kentucky basketball standout Anthony Epps, and the infamous Cornbread Mafia.

Artists & Athletes for TraumaPeople first

Growing up there taught George to put people first and make others feel welcome, values that have followed him through more than two decades in hospitality and continue to shape how he leads today.

Through that work, George connected with Laura Sharpe, founder of Artists for Trauma and Athletes for Trauma (AFT). Her mission resonated with the values that had shaped him long before his career at the Galt House: putting people first, creating opportunities, and helping others move forward.

“Sometimes what people need most is simply someone willing to believe in them and give them an opportunity.”
Lance George

The installationAFT x TRUST

For George, it was a natural connection between hospitality, community, and the work of AFT.

Today, those relationships have a visible home inside the Galt House through an AFT x TRUST art installation in the hotel lobby.

LouisvilleBuilding on the legacy

With guests from Louisville, across the country, and around the world moving through the space, the installation gives the artwork and its message a larger audience. It also reflects George’s belief that the Galt House can be more than a place to stay or attend a meeting. It can be a place where art, people, ideas, and stories come together.

For George, building on the Galt House legacy means continuing to expand what the hotel can contribute to Louisville. From welcoming its first MMA event to creating space for art and community partnerships, he wants the Galt House to support local organizations, spotlight Kentucky talent, and create opportunities for others.

“I want to leave Louisville better than I found it, and inspire the next generation to take it even further.”
Lance George

Ultimately, George hopes his contribution will be measured not only by what the Galt House accomplished, but by the people it helped and the opportunities it created.
