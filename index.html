Laura Sharpe's Artist Profile · Faith · Family · Music

Quan.B: from the football field to the octagon

Years after an injury closed the door on the athletic future Quan had imagined, music brought him back into an arena. The Louisville R&B and gospel singer sang the national anthem at LFA 235, where Artists for Trauma and Athletes for Trauma were sponsors.

Fight night · Louisville, Kentucky

Proud sponsors of LFA 235

Artists for Trauma and Athletes for Trauma were honored to serve as proud sponsors of LFA 235, where Founder Laura Sharpe and TRUST Agency Founder Eddie Donaldson joined athletes, artists, partners and supporters for an inspiring evening of connection and purpose.

LFA 235 delivered unforgettable moments. The evening featured Jake Woodley's record-setting 3.6-second knockout, the fastest finish in LFA history, along with Zayne Havener's second-round submission victory in the main event. Kestin McClain extended his undefeated record with a hard-fought unanimous decision.

More than a night of competition, LFA 235 reflected the courage, perseverance and human spirit that inspire our mission.

Race day · March 8, 2026

The Los Angeles Marathon is more than a race

It’s a living symbol of perseverance, determination and the extraordinary strength powered by human spirit.

At the 2026 ASICS Los Angeles Marathon, Artists for Trauma was honored to celebrate the Wheelchair Division and para athletes in collaboration with The McCourt Foundation, California Rehabilitation Foundation and California Rehabilitation Institute.

For many of these para athletes, the marathon represents more than 26.2 miles. It means recovery after trauma, the courage to rebuild, and the refusal to let life-altering challenges define the future.

Each athlete who crossed the finish line embodied what it means to be Powered by Human Spirit.

Your support turns hope into action.

Trauma can take away the life someone knew in an instant. Sport gives them a way back in. Your gift puts a bike, a pair of gloves or a running chair within reach, with a coach beside them who adapts to whatever they can do today. For many, it is the first time since their injury they have trained, competed, or felt like part of a team again.

Every program is free to veterans and civilian survivors, so we rely on people like you. Every donation honors the bravery, courage and tenacity of the survivors we serve.

When you give, you are not just funding a workshop. You are telling a survivor that their story did not end on the worst day of their life, and that there is still beauty, purpose and community waiting for them.

Your support turns recovery into an art

Every donation honors the bravery, courage and tenacity of the survivors we serve. It pays for the supplies, studios and teaching artists that make each workshop possible, and it keeps every program free of charge.

Artists For Trauma is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, EIN 45-4624556. Gifts are tax-deductible as allowed by law.

Creativity, connectivity, community

Founded in 2011 by trauma survivor Laura Sharpe, AFT pairs survivors with established artists for hands-on workshops in photography, filmmaking, painting, pottery, music, dance, writing, yoga and more.

Workshops happen in rehab hospitals, trauma centers, community spaces and public art projects, from Los Angeles to Louisville, alongside partners like the California Rehabilitation Institute and the Muhammad Ali Center.

How your donation is used

Zeffy is a free platform for nonprofits, so 100% of your gift reaches AFT. Here is where it goes.

  • Art suppliesPaint, clay, cameras and adaptive tools, so every survivor can take part whatever their range of motion.
  • Studio time and workshopsFree sessions led by working artists in hospitals, rehab centers and community spaces.
  • Survivors and familiesPrograms for civilian and military survivors of all ages, with families welcome.
  • Volunteer artistsTraining and support for the volunteers who bring AFT to new cities.
